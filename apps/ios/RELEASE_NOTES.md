Orionis Control 0.3.0 rebuilds the interface. The app now targets iOS 26 and adopts Liquid Glass where it earns its place: the controls that float over live video are drawn in the new material, so they sample the footage beneath them and stay legible over a bright driveway and a dark garage alike. They share a single effect container, because glass cannot sample glass and separate panes would read as unrelated chrome. The tab bar minimises as you scroll down the camera wall and the DNS feed, returning that space to the thing you are actually looking at.

Navigation is now five deliberate destinations — Home, Cameras, Network, System, More. Previously there were six, and a compact-width tab bar with six or more tabs makes UIKit generate its own overflow list, so the app was shipping a screen it neither designed nor controlled. Events and Settings moved into a real More hub that carries your name, role and live gateway connection state, reports honestly when the server has no detection capability rather than implying an empty feed, and exposes administration only to accounts that genuinely hold the permission behind it.

Cameras were reorganised around the camera. Status filters are an always-visible row with live counts instead of two levels into a toolbar menu, so "which cameras are down" is one tap. Camera detail leads with video, live state and recordings, and pushes model, firmware and stream diagnostics beneath them; the control row became real media controls rather than a row of bordered buttons. The full-screen viewer gained quality selection, which it never had, along with an offline state that names the camera and its last-seen time instead of showing an indefinite black frame, and a landscape layout that keeps the picture rather than portrait spacing.

DNS activity became a feed. The query log had given the domain, the client, the query type and the outcome roughly equal weight, so nothing could be scanned; the domain now leads and everything else is subordinate. Blocked queries are drawn in the accent colour rather than red, because a blocked query is the product working. Query detail and Insights came off stock forms — detail keeps network-wide rule changes visually separate from per-device actions, and Insights leads with its sample size, since every figure below it describes only the rows currently loaded. On iPad, activity and detail sit side by side instead of a sheet covering the feed you are reading it against.

System is an operations screen. Services rank by severity and split into what needs attention and what is running normally, healthy entries collapse to a single line, and a failing one explains what broke and what it costs you. Operation results moved from a list section halfway down the screen to a banner at the top. Infrastructure, Recording Storage and Signed-in Devices were brought onto the same design language, the last of these making the device you are holding unmistakable before you revoke anything.

Four defects are fixed. Deep links that arrived before their screen existed were silently dropped, which is exactly what happens when a notification launches a terminated app; the destination is now delivered on mount as well as on change. A failed camera control could render as a green success, so outcomes are typed now, and a request the camera declined reads as a warning rather than being claimed either way. Entering full screen raced the inline stream's teardown and could leave two gateway sessions open at once. And every screen that keeps its last good data through a failed refresh now says so, instead of presenting a stale camera wall or protection state as current.

WebRTC moves from M137 to M150. Live view, HLS fallback, reconnection, session revocation and the stream lifecycle are otherwise untouched — the presentation was the problem, not the media plane.
