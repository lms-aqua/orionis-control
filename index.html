Orionis Control Help & Support

Everything you need to set up and get the most out of Orionis Control — the one app for your cameras, network filtering and system health. Pick a topic on the left, or start with the basics below.

Overview

Orionis Control brings the things you actually check into a single app: whether your cameras are up, whether something moved in the driveway overnight, whether your network filtering is still doing its job, and whether everything underneath is healthy.

🎥 Cameras

Live video, snapshots, full-screen and Picture-in-Picture.

🕒 Recordings

A timeline you can scrub, filter and export clips from.

🛡️ Filtering

See and control DNS filtering — queries, blocks and rules.

📊 Health

Plain-language status for the services that matter.

🔔 Alerts

Push notifications for the things worth waking up for.

📱 Native

iPhone and iPad, portrait and landscape, light and dark.

This site is a guide to using the app. It does not cover installation of any server software or configuration behind the scenes.

Getting started

  1. Install the app on your iPhone or iPad and open it.
  2. Enter your gateway address when prompted — the web address you were given for your system. The app checks it and tells you right away if it can’t reach it.
  3. Sign in. You’ll use your normal account and, where your system requires it, a second step to confirm it’s you. Your session is kept only on your device and can be signed out at any time.
  4. You’re in. Cameras appear on the main wall, grouped by where they came from. Explore the tabs for recordings, filtering, health and settings.

Nothing to see yet? If no cameras appear, add a source under Settings → Connections — see Adding camera sources.

Cameras & live view

The camera wall shows every camera your system knows about, discovered for you — there’s no list to hand-maintain. Tap a camera to open it.

  • Live video with a low-latency stream, plus a snapshot you can grab at any moment.
  • Full screen & landscape, and Picture-in-Picture so a feed can keep playing while you do something else.
  • Camera controls — pan, tilt, zoom, lighting, presets and privacy — appear only for cameras that actually support them, so you’re never shown a control that does nothing.
  • Honest status. A camera that’s offline says so, and says why, instead of showing a frozen or blank image. A camera that’s recording shows a recording badge.

Two-way talk and some advanced controls depend on the camera and may not be available for every source.

Recordings & timeline

The recordings timeline lets you move through the day and see exactly where footage exists and where there are gaps.

  • Scrub the timeline to any moment; playback seeks straight there rather than only to fixed boundaries.
  • Filter by camera, date and event type — motion, person, vehicle, package, audio and system events — where your cameras report them.
  • Export a clip to save or share. Clips are pulled for the window you choose.
  • Storage at a glance. The app shows how much space recordings use and roughly how long footage will be kept, when that information is available.

“Nothing was recorded on this day.” If you expect footage and don’t see it, the recorder for that camera may not be set up, or it may have no footage in the range you’re viewing. See Troubleshooting.

Adding camera sources

Add the systems your cameras live behind under Settings → Connections. Each source states what it can actually do before you add it, and the app hides what it can’t — a source with no event history won’t show an empty Events tab.

To add one, tap Add a source, choose the kind, and fill in the fields it asks for (usually an address and a login). The app checks the source the moment you save, so you find out right away whether it worked.

Signing in to Blink

Add your Blink email and password, then enter the verification code Blink sends you. You do this once, in the app — after that the connection is remembered and Blink won’t keep asking. If a source needs a small helper service to run, the app offers to set it up for you after you’ve signed in; just tap Set up when prompted.

Managing sources is limited to administrator accounts, and every change is recorded.

Network filtering

A first-class view of your DNS filtering, not a web page in a costume.

  • See the picture: query volume, block rates, top clients and domains, and filter-list status across the time range you pick.
  • Search the query log with real filtering to find exactly what you’re looking for.
  • Allow and block rules that are checked for validity before they’re applied.
  • Pause protection deliberately — with a confirmation, a chosen duration, a visible reminder while it’s off, and a record of who paused it and why.

System health

Aggregate status for the services that matter, in plain language: healthy, warning, critical, offline or unknown — never colour alone, so it’s readable at a glance and with assistive tools.

A short list of safe, pre-approved actions is available where your system allows them — for example, re-running a health check or refreshing filters — rather than a command prompt in your pocket.

Notifications

Push alerts for the things worth knowing about — a camera dropping offline, a person detected, a recording problem, storage running low, or protection being turned off.

  • Tune them per camera, per event type and per severity.
  • Quiet hours keep the non-urgent ones from waking you.
  • Deep links: tapping a notification opens the exact screen it’s about.

Make sure notifications are allowed for Orionis Control in your device’s Settings, or alerts won’t reach you.

Troubleshooting

A camera shows as offline

The camera isn’t sending video right now. It usually comes back on its own once it reconnects. Check the camera’s power and network; the app will show it as online again automatically.

Live view won’t play

Try backing out and reopening the camera. If it still won’t play, confirm the camera shows as online, and that your device has a working connection to your system.

A camera is recording but doesn’t show a recording badge

The badge appears once the app can confirm fresh footage is being written. If a camera is genuinely recording and the badge is missing, make sure the recorder for that source is set up and reachable.

Blink keeps asking for a verification code

You only need to enter a code once, when you first sign in to Blink in the app. Finish that sign-in step before setting anything else up. Once the connection is signed in, the code prompts stop.

“Nothing was recorded on this day”

Either that camera has no footage in the range you’re viewing, or its recorder isn’t configured. Try a different date, and confirm the source is set up to record.

Can’t reach the gateway

Double-check the address you entered and that your device is on a network that can reach your system. The app tells you plainly when it can’t connect rather than hanging.

Frequently asked questions

Which devices does the app run on?

iPhone and iPad. iPhone, iPhone landscape, iPad and iPad split-screen are all first-class layouts, in light and dark appearance.

What cameras and systems are supported?

Frigate, UniFi Protect, Scrypted, TP-Link Tapo, Reolink, Dahua and Amcrest, Hikvision, Axis, Foscam, VIVOTEK, Wyze, Uniview, Hanwha (Wisenet), Blink, Ring, Eufy, Arlo, Google Nest, any ONVIF camera, and generic RTSP / go2rtc sources. See Adding camera sources. Each states what it can do before you add it.

Can I use it away from home?

Yes, as long as your device can reach your system’s gateway address. How that’s reachable from outside your home is part of how your system is set up.

Is my footage or data sent anywhere?

The app talks to your own system. It isn’t a cloud service that stores your video for you. See Privacy & security.

Why can’t I add or change a source?

Managing camera sources and other sensitive actions are limited to administrator accounts. If you need access, ask whoever administers your system.

Why don’t I see an Events or Recordings tab for a camera?

The app only shows what a source can actually do. If a source has no event history or recordings, those tabs are hidden rather than shown empty — an empty list would wrongly read as “nothing happened.”

Privacy & security

Orionis Control is built to talk to your system, not a third-party cloud.

  • Your credentials stay yours. Any passwords or account details you enter for a camera source are stored securely and are never shown back to the app once saved.
  • Secure sign-in. You sign in with your own account, and your session lives only on your device — you can sign out at any time.
  • Least privilege. Sensitive actions — managing sources, pausing protection, service actions — are limited to administrators and are recorded.
  • Honest states. When something is offline, degraded or unavailable, the app says so clearly instead of pretending.
